# Feasibility — can these two cameras actually track a golf ball?

Short answer: **yes, with the IR strobe — and provably no without it.** This
page is the quantitative proof, generated by `scripts/feasibility_report.py`
(which calls `golfsim/feasibility.py`). Re‑run it yourself:

```bash
python scripts/feasibility_report.py
```

## Recommended rig — Pi Global Shutter + 6 mm lens + 5‑pulse IR strobe

`HFOV 45° · ground sampling 0.72 mm/px · 5 pulses/frame · cameras ~1.25 m out`

| ball speed | samples | travel/pulse | motion blur | ball Ø | speed res. | verdict |
|-----------:|--------:|-------------:|------------:|-------:|-----------:|---------|
| 70 mph (wedge) | 5.8 | 4.1 cm / 57 px | 0.5 px | 59 px | ±0.16 % | **FEASIBLE** |
| 95 mph | 5.0 | 5.5 cm / 77 px | 0.7 px | 59 px | ±0.14 % | **FEASIBLE** |
| 130 mph | 5.0 | 7.6 cm / 105 px | 1.0 px | 59 px | ±0.10 % | **FEASIBLE** |
| 167 mph (tour) | 5.0 | 9.7 cm / 135 px | 1.2 px | 59 px | ±0.08 % | **FEASIBLE** |
| 183 mph (long) | 5.0 | 10.6 cm / 148 px | 1.4 px | 59 px | ±0.08 % | **FEASIBLE** |

Every speed clears all three gates: **≥ 3 samples** for a velocity fit,
**motion blur < ⅓ of the ball** so centroids stay sharp, and **ball ≥ 6 px**
so it's detectable. Speed resolution stays well under 0.2 %.

## Counter‑example — 1080p60 webcam, no strobe

`HFOV 75° · ground sampling 1.0 mm/px · 1 frame/exposure`

| ball speed | samples | travel/frame | motion blur | verdict |
|-----------:|--------:|-------------:|------------:|---------|
| 95 mph | 2.6 | 74 cm / 925 px | 222 px | **NOT FEASIBLE** |
| 167 mph | 2.0 | 124 cm / 1545 px | 371 px | **NOT FEASIBLE** |

It fails on two independent counts: too few samples (the ball clears the zone
in ~one frame) and catastrophic motion blur (the ball is a 20–30 cm streak).
No amount of clever software recovers a velocity from this. This is exactly why
the build spends its money on a **global shutter + strobe**, not on a
higher‑megapixel webcam.

## The three gates, explained

1. **Samples ≥ 3.** A constant‑acceleration fit needs ≥ 3 points per axis.
   The strobe manufactures 5 from a single frame; a plain camera gets ~1–2.
2. **Motion blur < ball/3.** Blur = `speed × exposure`. The 12 µs strobe pulse
   keeps it ~1 px even at 183 mph; a 4 ms webcam exposure makes it hundreds.
3. **Ball ≥ 6 px.** Sets a maximum useful distance / minimum focal length for
   reliable sub‑pixel centroids. At ~1.25 m with a 6 mm lens the ball is ~59 px
   — plenty.

## Speed‑resolution sanity check

With ~0.3 px sub‑pixel centroid error and a 5‑sample baseline spanning
~16–42 cm of travel (4 intervals × 4.1–10.6 cm depending on ball speed), the
fractional speed error is roughly
`√2 · 0.3 px ÷ (total displacement in px)` ≈ **0.1 %** — i.e. ±0.1–0.2 mph on a
167 mph drive. The Monte‑Carlo test `test_noise_sensitivity_speed_resolution`
confirms <1 % average error under 0.3 px jitter across 20 random shots.

## What this does and doesn't prove

- **Proves:** the optics/timing/geometry budget closes with parts you can buy
  for ~$360, and the maths recovers the right numbers under realistic noise.
- **Doesn't prove:** that *your* lens is in focus, *your* strobe is bright
  enough, or *your* calibration is tight. Those are build‑quality issues the
  docs walk you through — the physics headroom (1.2 px blur vs a 59 px ball)
  is generous enough to absorb imperfect execution.
